# M2.0A — Character design audit

Status: **approved.** Expressive eyes are in [M2.0B](32_M2.0B_EXPRESSIVE_EYES.md). This note is the audit that preceded that work.

The validated M1.9 robot stays the identity: a small white body, a glossy black face, neon-green features, and a tilted antenna, inside a 300×400 DIP transparent overlay. Expressiveness today is a handful of scalar offsets on that rig. Sleep is the only pose that reads immediately. Happy is the next clearest. Listening, thinking, responding, speaking, and error are easy to confuse with each other.

## 1. Current character architecture

The renderer owns one `WebGLRenderer` in `createCompanionScene` (`src/renderer/avatar/createScene.ts`). The trail window is a separate Canvas 2D `BrowserWindow` and is outside this rig. Window travel stays in the main process (`setBounds` in `src/main/overlayWindow.ts`). The scene graph never moves the window.

### Geometry and proportions

`createProceduralRobot` (`src/renderer/avatar/proceduralRobot.ts`) builds the rig once. Node names are the contract noted for a later GLB.

| Node | Construction |
| --- | --- |
| `Root` → `Hover` → pivot | Hover offset, tilt, yaw, and wake scale |
| `Main body` | `SphereGeometry(1, 64, 48)`, scale `(1.03, 0.98, 0.99)` |
| `Face display` | Group at `(0, 0.04, 0.56)` |
| Face lens | `SphereGeometry(0.72, 48, 36)`, scale `(1.22, 0.96, 0.66)`, glossy black |
| `Left eye`, `Right eye` | Shared unlit torus arc, radius `0.15`, tube `0.06`, arc `1.22π`. Placed at `x = ±0.26`, `y = 0.08`, `z = 0.46` |
| Catchlights | `SphereGeometry(0.026)` on each eye, mirrored in X |
| `Cheek lights` | Two squashed spheres, radius `0.07`, scale `(1.45, 0.72, 0.45)`, at `x = ±0.4`, `y = -0.24` |
| `Antenna base` | Cylinder stem length `0.26`, sphere tip radius `0.15`, rest roll `0.12` rad. One `PointLight` on the tip |
| `Left arm`, `Right arm` | Shoulder disc, neon ring, cap, capsule upper arm, elbow group, capsule forearm, sphere hand |

Proportions live in `src/shared/robotFrame.ts`: body radius `1`, antenna root `y = 0.9`, shoulders at `x = ±1.08`. `cameraDistanceForBody` frames the body sphere to the 160 DIP click circle (`ORB_DIAMETER_DIP` in `src/shared/clickThrough.ts`). `rigFitsFrame` checks that hover, tilt, antenna, and arms stay inside the 300×400 view. Arms and the antenna are allowed to draw outside the click circle. They are not hit targets (`HIT_SLACK_DIP` is `0`).

### Materials and color

| Role | Material | Hex |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shell (body, stem, arms, hands) | `MeshPhysicalMaterial`, roughness `0.38`, clearcoat `0.45`, metalness `0.02` | `0xf3f0ea` |
| Face lens | `MeshPhysicalMaterial`, roughness `0.07`, clearcoat `1`, metalness `0.08` | `0x07080c` |
| Eyes and shoulder rings | `MeshBasicMaterial` | `0xb6ff3a` |
| Cheeks | `MeshBasicMaterial` | `0x93e85a` |
| Antenna tip | `MeshBasicMaterial` | `0xc6ff55` |
| Catchlights | `MeshBasicMaterial` | white, then `setScalar(glow)` |

There is no emissive map and no bloom. Neon is an unlit color. `paint()` multiplies that color by `pose.glow` every apply. The tip light is `intensity = 0.16 * glow`, distance `1.8`, decay `2`. Scene lights, created once in `createCompanionScene`: hemisphere `0.72`, warm key `1.45`, cool fill `0.38`. Output is `SRGBColorSpace`, `ACESFilmicToneMapping`, exposure `1.05`. Tone mapping also shifts the unlit greens.

### Face and eyes

`createEye` makes one arc plus one catchlight. Both eyes share `eyeMaterial`. `apply()` writes the same pitch, the same yaw, and the same vertical scale to both. The only asymmetry is a mirrored Z roll, `cheer = (eyeScale - 1) * 0.45`. There is no pupil, no eyelid mesh, and no morph target.

Blink is a squash. `shut = (1 - blink * 0.88) * eyeScale`, applied to `scale.y`. At `blink = 1` the arch is still `0.12` tall. Catchlights hide when `blink >= 0.72`. A blink therefore flattens the smile instead of closing a lid.

### Animation loop

`createCharacterDirector` (`src/renderer/avatar/characterDirector.ts`) is the clock. `step()`:

1. `stepBehavior` (`src/shared/behavior.ts`) advances `SLEEPING`, `WAKING`, `IDLE`, `TRACKING`, `RETURNING_TO_SLEEP`.
2. `gazeTarget` / `stepGaze` (`src/shared/gaze.ts`) ease the look toward the cursor, or back to rest.
3. `ambientPose` (`src/shared/ambientMotion.ts`) builds hover, tilt, antenna sway, and a periodic blink.
4. `presentCharacter` (`src/shared/presentation.ts`) blends that idle pose with alertness and gaze.
5. `applyPresence` (`src/shared/presence.ts`) offsets the awake pose.
6. `withGesture` adds `greet`, `nod`, or `explain` from `src/shared/gesturePose.ts`.
7. `applyTravelPose` (`src/shared/desktopMovement.ts`) adds a small lean while the window is traveling.

`createCompanionScene` drives this with `renderer.setAnimationLoop`. The loop detaches when `document.hidden`. While `SLEEPING`, `shouldRenderFrame` allows a GPU render only every `SLEEP_FRAME_MS` (`80`). The director still steps on every callback. Awake frames always render. A playing gesture also forces a render during sleep.

`AvatarAdapter` (`src/renderer/avatar/avatarAdapter.ts`) is the seam: `apply`, `pulse`, `dispose`. The procedural rig is the only implementation.

### Gaze, sleep, and wake

Gaze uses a 520 DIP range, yaw limit `0.38` rad, pitch limit `0.2` rad, and a 90 ms exponential ease (`GAZE_RESPONSE_MS`). Cursor samples never wake the robot and never move the 60-second idle deadline. Tracking ends 400 ms after the sample goes stale (`SAMPLE_STALE_MS` is 250 ms). Body yaw takes 38% of gaze yaw. Body pitch takes 28% of gaze pitch. Eyes take the full awake gaze.

Sleep and wake are alertness blends, not separate clips. Wake is 700 ms with `easeOutBack` (`wakeBounce` feeds `wakeScale`). Return to sleep is 800 ms smoothstep. Full sleep sets glow `0.18`, nod `0.18`, roll `0.08`, antenna droop `0.42`, arms relaxed, eyes shut, and hover reduced to 20%. `applyPresence` returns the pose unchanged while `alertness < 0.9`, so conversational faces stay off through sleep and most of the wake.

### How chat, voice, and Qwen reach the face

Presence names are `ATTENTIVE`, `THINKING`, `RESPONDING`, `SPEAKING`, `HAPPY`, `ERROR`, `IDLE`. There is no `CURIOUS` or `LISTENING` name.

`createConversationSession` (`src/shared/conversationSession.ts`) publishes the turn:

- Send starts at `ATTENTIVE`, then `THINKING`.
- The first streamed token switches to `RESPONDING`.
- A finished reply calls `showBriefly('HAPPY')` for 1600 ms, then `IDLE`.
- A failed reply calls `showBriefly('ERROR')` for 2400 ms, then `IDLE`.
- Demo replies also emit `greet`, `nod`, or `explain` (`src/shared/demoConversation.ts`). Qwen turns do not emit those gestures.

`overlayForVoicePhase` (`src/shared/voiceSession.ts`) maps microphone `RECORDING` to `ATTENTIVE` and `TRANSCRIBING` to `THINKING`. Speaking stays on the TTS path.

`publishOverlayPresence` in `src/main/chatWindow.ts` resolves one value: TTS speaking wins, otherwise the voice pose, otherwise the session presence. Pablo playback sets `ttsSpeaking` through `chat:tts-presence`. Closing the chat calls `session.stop()`, clears the TTS and voice flags, then publishes whatever `lastSessionPresence` still is. `stop()` only aborts the turn. The face becomes `IDLE` later, when that turn's `finally` publishes. A happy or error timer can still be in flight across the hide.

`characterDirector.sleep` does not clear `presence` or `gesture`. The stored face comes back once alertness passes `0.9`.

### Window, click-through, and pixel ratio

`createOverlayChrome` (`src/shared/overlayOptions.ts`): frameless, transparent, `alwaysOnTop` level `floating`, `focusable: false`, `hasShadow: false`, `roundedCorners: false`, `show: false`. The overlay then calls `setBackgroundMaterial('none')`, `setBackgroundColor('#00000000')`, and `showInactive()`. Click-through is main-process only: `setIgnoreMouseEvents` starts true and tracks the 160 DIP body circle on a 16 ms poll. The renderer cannot call it.

`createCompanionScene` caps `devicePixelRatio` at `2`, with `antialias: true`, `alpha: true`, `premultipliedAlpha: false`, and `powerPreference: 'high-performance'`. The page background is transparent (`src/renderer/styles.css`). The chat button is an HTML control at `(244, 314)`, outside the body, shown only while the robot is awake.

### What already costs CPU and GPU

- One transparent always-on-top window. Desktop composition of that window is a standing cost on Windows.
- The animation callback runs at display rate whenever the overlay is visible, including sleep. Sleep only skips `renderer.render` down to about 12.5 fps.
- Awake renders every callback: clearcoat physical materials, filmic tone mapping, MSAA, and a transparent framebuffer.
- Body sphere segments are `64×48` and the face lens is `48×36`. That is thousands of triangles for a body drawn at about 160 CSS pixels.
- About twenty meshes, one draw each. Materials and geometries are created once and disposed with the adapter. `apply()` does not allocate meshes.
- `step()` does allocate several pose objects per callback (`ambientPose`, `presentCharacter`, `applyPresence`, `withGesture`, `applyTravelPose`), and it calls `matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)')` on that same cadence.
- `paint()` rewrites unlit colors every apply.
- The tip `PointLight` is the only per-character light beyond the three scene lights. Shadows are off. There is no postprocessing pass and no second WebGL context.
- `powerPreference: 'high-performance'` asks the GPU not to stay in its lowest power state. On Iris Xe that is the only GPU.

`prefers-reduced-motion` already removes presence pulses, travel lean, and gestures. The idle hover, tilt, antenna sway, and blink in `ambientPose` keep running.

## 2. Technical limitations

Eyes cannot change shape independently today, even though each eye is its own `Group` and could take its own `scale`. They share one material, one blink value, and one `eyeScale`. Shape vocabulary is uniform scale plus a mirrored roll of the same torus.

Blink cannot close. Squashing a thick arc never reads as a lid, and the catchlight pop at `0.72` is a hard cut.

There is no micro-saccade. Gaze is one smoothed cursor target. At rest the eyes are still, so the face looks held even while the body hovers.

`eyeScale` is the only expression control on the face, and `presentCharacter` hardcodes it to `1`. Presence then overwrites it for thinking (`0.84`), speaking (`1.04`), and happy (`1.16`). Cheeks and the antenna tip only brighten or dim with the shared `glow`. They never change shape.

Thinking adds `0.42` to blink and caps it at `0.55`, then multiplies by `0.84`. That is a heavy squash. Next to sleep, which forces blink to `1`, thinking looks drowsy. Error is the same green, slightly dimmer (`glow * 0.72`), with a small extra blink. Responding is a 6% glow change and an 8-thousandths hover pulse. Speaking is only a little larger. Attentive, which is also the listening pose, is a 5% glow lift and a slightly perkier antenna.

Curious has no signal. The nearest behavior is `TRACKING`, and that only turns the eyes and a fraction of the body. It does not change the face.

Gestures ignore alertness. A `greet` started just before sleep still offsets tilt and antenna, and it forces full-rate rendering until the 700 ms envelope ends.

Hide can republish a stale session face. `publishOverlayPresence` runs before the aborted turn settles, so `THINKING`, `HAPPY`, or `ERROR` can remain on an awake robot after the chat window is gone. Sleep hides that face only while alertness stays under `0.9`.

Secondary motion is safe for window travel only while amplitudes stay inside `rigFitsFrame`. The click circle will not grow to follow an arm. A bigger arm swing would look pressable and would not be.

Ambient motion is deterministic (`Math.sin` of `timeMs`). Any new idle motion should stay that way so the existing pose tests keep a fixed clock.

## 3. Recommended visual direction

Keep the white shell, the black lens, the neon-green family (`0xb6ff3a`, `0x93e85a`, `0xc6ff55`), and the antenna. Do not add ears, a hat, a backpack, or a mouth in M2.0B–E. A mouth would be the first new facial feature, and speaking can read through the antenna and cheeks until eyes have been tried.

Improve the face by giving the existing arcs a lid and a few silhouettes:

- Rest: the current open arch.
- Blink and sleep: a lid, or a scale that actually reaches a closed line, with the catchlight fading instead of popping.
- Listening (`ATTENTIVE`): arches a little taller, antenna lifted, cheeks steady.
- Curious: a short derived pose while `TRACKING` and no conversational presence is active. Slightly wider eyes, a small head tilt toward the cursor. It ends when the gaze returns to rest. It must not wake, open chat, or move the window.
- Thinking: eyes narrowed and aimed a little upward, antenna drawing a slow small arc, glow held up so it cannot be mistaken for sleep.
- Responding: the thinking silhouette with a slow cheek pulse for as long as tokens are streaming.
- Speaking: the open arch plus a small antenna bob tied to the TTS flag. No lip sync.
- Happy: taller arches, cheeks lifted by scale, the existing 1600 ms life. The current overshoot scale can stay small.
- Error: the same green, a dropped antenna, a slower blink, and a slight roll. Color stays in the green family so the identity holds. Duration stays 2400 ms.

Body motion stays secondary and small. Idle hover stays near `0.04` world units. Antenna sway stays near `0.12` rad. Arms stay close to the body. Sleep droop, wake overshoot, and the travel lean remain the large poses, and travel still yields to speaking, error, and happy.

Timing stays on the clocks already in the pure modules. New envelopes use `smoothstep` or the existing sine. Reduced motion should also quiet `ambientPose` once M2.0C touches that file: keep a static awake pose and a working blink, and drop hover, sway, and travel lean.

Materials stay on the current renderer. A one-time softer highlight on the lens is the polish to try in M2.0E, after measuring. Do not add a bloom pass to make the green "more neon."

## 4. Expression matrix

| Desired read | Signal today | What the face does | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IDLE | Phase `IDLE` or `TRACKING`, presence `null` / `IDLE` | Hover, tilt, antenna sway, squash-blink every 3400 ms | Works. Blink is the weak part |
| CURIOUS | Phase `TRACKING` only | Eyes and a little body follow the cursor | Missing as a face. Derive it. Do not add an IPC name |
| LISTENING | Voice `RECORDING` → `ATTENTIVE` | Glow `×1.05`, antenna `-0.04`, blink slightly reduced | Ambiguous with idle |
| THINKING | Session `THINKING`, or voice `TRANSCRIBING` | Squash toward shut, `eyeScale 0.84`, antenna down, extra pitch | Readable and too close to sleep |
| RESPONDING | First Qwen or demo token → `RESPONDING` | Glow `×1.06`, hover pulse `0.008` | Too weak to see at 160 DIP |
| SPEAKING | `ttsSpeaking` overrides the others | Glow `×1.08`, `eyeScale 1.04`, small antenna and hover pulse | Too close to responding |
| HAPPY | `showBriefly('HAPPY')`, 1600 ms | Glow up to `1.45`, `eyeScale 1.16`, blink forced open, scale `1.05`, antenna perked | Clearest conversational pose. Still the same arch |
| ERROR | `showBriefly('ERROR')`, 2400 ms | Glow `×0.72`, extra blink, antenna `+0.16` | Ambiguous dim idle. Keep it green |
| SLEEPING | Alertness `0`, including the 800 ms return | Shut eyes, dim glow `0.18`, nod, roll, antenna droop `0.42`, loose arms | Strong. Leave the timing alone |

Priority to implement in the director, highest first:

1. `SLEEPING` and `RETURNING_TO_SLEEP` draw the sleep blend only. A gesture in progress is cleared. Presence is kept in memory and is not drawn.
2. `WAKING` below alertness `0.9` draws the wake blend only. This is the current `applyPresence` guard.
3. `SPEAKING` while TTS is actually playing.
4. `ATTENTIVE` while the microphone is recording. That is listening.
5. `THINKING`, including transcription and the pre-token wait.
6. `RESPONDING` while tokens are streaming.
7. `HAPPY` and `ERROR` for their existing brief windows.
8. Curious while tracking and no presence above is active.
9. Idle micro-motion.

Idle motion, curious, blink, and saccades must not call `wake`, `notifyCircleClick`, `requestOpenChat`, or any wander API. Stop, close, and sleep must not leave happy, error, or a gesture on screen. Close should publish `CLEAR` immediately from `hide()`, and a late settle must not put `ERROR` back on a hidden chat. If the robot wakes and the chat is still honestly thinking or speaking, that face may return. A happy or error interval that already expired stays idle.

## 5. Proposed controller design

Keep the split that already exists. The pure modules stay free of Three.js. The adapter stays the only mesh writer.

| Role | Module |
| --- | --- |
| Character visual owner | `createCharacterDirector` in `src/renderer/avatar/characterDirector.ts` |
| Face expressions | New pure `src/shared/faceExpression.ts`, called from `applyPresence` or from the director in its place |
| Gaze | `src/shared/gaze.ts` (`gazeTarget`, `stepGaze`) |
| Secondary motion | `ambientPose`, `presentCharacter`, `gestureOffset`, `applyTravelPose` |
| State mapping | Existing publishers only: `conversationSession`, `overlayForVoicePhase`, `publishOverlayPresence` |
| Mesh output | `createProceduralRobot` `apply()` |

`faceExpression` returns numbers the rig already understands, plus the few new ones M2.0B needs: `eyelid`, `eyeScaleLeft`, `eyeScaleRight`, `cheekScale`, `gazeJitterYaw`, `gazeJitterPitch`. Defaults match today's pose so current tests keep their meaning.

`GazeController` stays `stepGaze`. Micro-saccades are a second pure function of `timeMs`, bounded to a few hundredths of a radian, added after the cursor ease. Use a sine, not `Math.random`, so the motion is reproducible. They turn off while sleeping and while reduced motion is on.

`SecondaryMotionController` is not a new class. Antenna phase, arm relax, and hover remain in `ambientMotion.ts` and `presentation.ts`. `apply()` already writes antenna `rotation` and both elbow groups. Those rotations do not change `BrowserWindow` bounds.

Curious is computed in the director from `phase === 'TRACKING'` and a null or `IDLE` presence. No new channel, no tray item, no model call.

M2.0D should introduce one pure resolver, for example `resolveFaceCue(input)`, so the interruption list in section 4 is unit-tested without Electron. `chatWindow.hide` and `characterDirector.sleep` call it. They do not grow new knowledge of Qwen, Whisper, or Pablo.

## 6. Performance budget and measurement

Hardware target: Windows 11, Surface Laptop Go 3, Intel Core i5-1235U, 8 GB RAM, Intel Iris Xe. This audit did not profile that machine. The numbers below are budgets for M2.0E to confirm, not measurements.

Stay on the one renderer in `createCompanionScene`. Reuse the geometries and materials created in `createProceduralRobot`. Do not add `EffectComposer`, a bloom pass, shadow maps, a second canvas, or an animation library. `three` `0.186.1` is already the renderer.

| Budget | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| WebGL contexts for the robot | 1 |
| Pixel ratio | Current cap of `2` |
| New meshes after load | 0 per frame. Eyelids, if added, are created once in `createEye` |
| Draw calls | One per existing mesh, plus at most two eyelid meshes |
| Triangles in M2.0B and M2.0C | No increase beyond those optional lids |
| Sleep GPU renders | At most one every `80` ms, unchanged |
| Awake JS | No `matchMedia` and no geometry or material construction inside `step` or `apply` |
| Lights | The current hemisphere, key, fill, and one tip light |
| Window | 300×400 DIP, 160 DIP click circle, transparent clear color `alpha 0` |

M2.0B and M2.0C should not retune segment counts or `powerPreference`. Those are M2.0E experiments, each reversible on its own.

Measurement:

- Development, once at startup: the existing log `desktop-companion: webgl ready calls=… triangles=… dpr=… gpu=…`. Record it before M2.0B and after each later milestone. Iris Xe should appear in `gpu`.
- Development, during M2.0E: a log gated by the existing dev flag, at most once a minute, with `renderer.info.render.calls`, `renderer.info.render.triangles`, and the average callback interval. Reset the info counters after the sample. No on-screen HUD.
- Production and the laptop check: Task Manager, the Electron GPU engine, for three quiet minutes asleep, three minutes awake and still, one wander session, and one chat turn with speech. Watch CPU of the app process as well. Sleep should sit below awake idle. Awake idle should stay a small slice of Iris Xe while the robot is the only moving window.
- Failure line: a change that keeps the loop rendering full rate during `SLEEPING`, that allocates geometries per frame, or that adds a second renderer.

`powerPreference: 'high-performance'` stays until that laptop check. If idle GPU time is high while the pose is almost still, M2.0E may try the default preference on a branch that can be reverted without touching the rig.

## 7. Milestones

Each milestone keeps movement, Neon Trail, click-through, the six-minute lease, sleep, Qwen, Whisper, and Pablo behavior. No new packages. No GLB until M2.0F says yes.

### M2.0B — Expressive eyes

Scope: closed blink, independent eye scale, deterministic micro-saccades, and distinct silhouettes for idle, listening, thinking, happy, error, and sleep. Optional eyelid meshes created once. Curious can wait for M2.0D if it needs the resolver.

Modules: `proceduralRobot.ts` (`createEye`, `apply`), `ambientMotion.ts` (`AvatarPose`, `blinkAmount`), `presentation.ts`, `presence.ts` or new `faceExpression.ts`, `gaze.ts`, `characterDirector.ts` only to pass the new fields through.

Risks: a lid intersecting the black lens; catchlights popping; sleep tests that assume `blink` still means a squash; any change to the click circle or the camera distance.

Tests: blink reaches a closed value; left and right scales can differ; saccade stays inside a fixed bound and is zero when asleep; `applyPresence` still returns the input pose below alertness `0.9`; happy `eyeScale` stays above `1` and thinking stays below `1`; reduced motion disables saccades. Existing behavior, gaze, and presence tests stay green.

Manual: sleep and wake at the current 700 ms and 800 ms; eyes follow the cursor; thinking and happy are distinguishable from across a desk; corners of the window still click through; Move and Neon Trail still work.

Acceptance: a blink reads as a blink; sleep eyes are shut; thinking is narrowed and still bright; happy is the wide face; no new package and no triangle growth beyond the optional lids.

Rollback: revert the eye block in `apply()` and the new pose fields. The torus rig is the fallback.

### M2.0C — Secondary body motion

Scope: antenna, arms, and head poses that support the faces from M2.0B. Idle personality stays inside the current hover and sway magnitudes. `rigFitsFrame` must pass with the new extremes. Reduced motion quiets hover, sway, and travel lean.

Modules: `ambientMotion.ts`, `presentation.ts`, `gesturePose.ts`, `applyTravelPose` in `desktopMovement.ts`, antenna and arm writes in `proceduralRobot.ts`. `robotFrame.ts` only if a constant used by `rigFitsFrame` changes.

Risks: antenna or hands clipped by the 300×400 window; arms swinging over the desktop where clicks do not land; wander plus body sway feeling like the window is shaking; gestures stacking on the new antenna curve.

Tests: `rigFitsFrame` with the hover and tilt used at runtime; gesture offsets still return `null` after their durations; `applyTravelPose` still no-ops when asleep, reduced, or when presence is `SPEAKING`, `ERROR`, or `HAPPY`.

Manual: wander for a full six-minute lease, Return Home, Stop, Neon Trail, sleep droop, and a demo `greet` / `nod` / `explain`. The window bounds must not change because an arm moved.

Acceptance: the robot feels less rigid while still. Travel, trail, and click-through match M1.9. Nothing new sticks out of the window.

Rollback: restore the previous amplitudes in `ambientMotion.ts` and `presentation.ts`. Mesh topology stays.

### M2.0D — Expression state integration

Scope: the priority list in section 4, including a derived curious pose. Clear gestures on sleep. Publish `CLEAR` from chat hide before a late turn settle, and ignore a settle that belongs to a hidden chat. Speaking remains the TTS flag. Listening remains voice `ATTENTIVE`. Do not change request payloads, Whisper, Pablo voice selection, or Qwen streaming.

Modules: `characterDirector.ts`, new `resolveFaceCue` in `faceExpression.ts`, `chatWindow.ts` `publishOverlayPresence` and `hide`. `conversationSession.ts` only if the happy and error timers need to cancel on hide; prefer handling that in `hide()` so the session timings stay `1600` and `2400`.

Risks: hiding a real in-flight `THINKING` that should return on wake; a late `ERROR` after close; curious firing during recording because presence was dropped; tests that assume `ATTENTIVE` still means the old tiny glow.

Tests: fake-clock resolver cases for all eight desired reads plus speaking; sleep during happy draws the sleep pose; wake after the happy window draws idle; TTS wins over `THINKING`; hide ends at `CLEAR`; cursor motion still does not wake or move `sleepDeadline`.

Manual: record, transcribe, a Qwen turn, Pablo playback, happy, forced error, Stop, close during happy, Sleep during thinking, then wake. No face left over after close or sleep.

Acceptance: each row of the matrix is visible once, and stop, close, and sleep leave the idle or sleep pose.

Rollback: director goes back to calling `applyPresence` directly. `hide()` goes back to the current publish order.

### M2.0E — Visual polish and performance

Scope: measure, then adjust. Candidates, each on its own: cache `matchMedia`; reuse one pose object in `step()`; rewrite unlit colors only when `glow` changes; lower body and lens segment counts if the startup triangle log is high and the silhouette still holds; a softer lens highlight; the `powerPreference` trial from section 6. No bloom, no shadow map, no second context.

Modules: `createScene.ts`, material setup in `proceduralRobot.ts`, the allocation sites in `characterDirector.ts`.

Risks: faceting the white body; dulling the black lens; a transparent-window regression if the clear color or premultiplied alpha changes; a power-preference change that is hard to see without the laptop check.

Tests: source guard that `createScene.ts` has one `WebGLRenderer`, `MAX_PIXEL_RATIO` of `2`, and no `EffectComposer`; `rigFitsFrame` still true; sleep frame interval constant unchanged; presence and behavior suites unchanged.

Manual: the three-minute sleep and awake samples on the Surface Laptop Go 3, plus one wander and one spoken reply. Confirm the log's `gpu` string, `dpr`, `calls`, and `triangles`. Confirm the four window corners still pass through and the trail still sits behind the body.

Acceptance: the recorded GPU and CPU samples meet the budgets in section 6, or the milestone stops on the specific change that missed them and reverts that change.

Rollback: each experiment reverts independently. The M2.0B eye rig and M2.0D resolver stay.

### M2.0F — GLB / Blender decision

Scope: a decision, not an import. Default recommendation is to keep the procedural rig.

Revisit a GLB only if M2.0B's eyelids and silhouettes fail the desk test at 160 DIP, or if M2.0E cannot hit the GPU budget without the body looking faceted. A future asset would still implement `AvatarAdapter`. The clock stays in `characterDirector` and the pure pose modules. Clips inside the file would not own sleep, gaze, or presence. Node names would match the groups already in `createProceduralRobot`. The file would load through the existing packaged-file protocol. `GLTFLoader` from `three` is not a new npm dependency, and it is still deferred: it grows the renderer bundle and adds a parser on the laptop we are trying to keep idle.

Risks of importing early: a second source of truth for proportions, a framing change that breaks the 160 DIP circle, textures that cost more than the procedural materials, and a loader path in the sandboxed renderer.

Tests if a GLB is ever approved: adapter contract, `rigFitsFrame`, click-circle framing, startup triangle and call counts no worse than the procedural baseline, and a missing-file fallback to `createProceduralRobot`.

Manual: the same Windows pass as M2.0E, plus hide, quit, and a context-restore.

Acceptance to adopt a GLB: it wins the desk test and the laptop GPU sample against the procedural rig. Otherwise the procedural rig remains the character.

## 8. Risks and open decisions

Open decisions, to settle before M2.0B:

1. Eyelids as two extra unlit meshes, or a closed scale on the current arcs with a fading catchlight. The mesh reads better. The scale touches fewer tests. Prefer the mesh only if a quick static check in B shows the squash still looks like a squint.
2. Error stays inside the neon-green palette. Confirm that a slumped antenna is enough, and that the face should not turn red.
3. No mouth through M2.0E. Speaking is antenna and cheek motion.
4. Reduced motion will also quiet idle hover once M2.0C lands. Blink can remain.
5. `powerPreference` stays `high-performance` until the M2.0E laptop sample.

Risks to watch across the phase:

- Any pose that changes `cameraDistanceForBody` or the body scale will desync the drawn sphere from the 160 DIP click circle.
- Any larger arm or antenna extreme can leave `rigFitsFrame` and clip at the overlay edge.
- Presence work in `chatWindow.ts` can stale-lock a face if `hide()` and the turn `finally` both publish. M2.0D owns that race.
- Rewriting `applyPresence` carelessly can let `THINKING` draw during sleep. The alertness guard stays.
- Segment reduction and clearcoat changes can erase the glossy black face. Measure before changing them.
- A GLB imported to "solve" expression would bypass the validated procedural contract. M2.0F is the gate.
